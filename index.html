Upper Extremity Physical Therapy Review

1. Test to confirm bicipital tendonitis
Speed’s test
2. Define bursitis
Inflammation of a bursa, a fluid-filled sac that reduces friction between moving tissues.
3. How many tendons pass through the carpal tunnel? Name them.
Nine tendons: four from flexor digitorum superficialis, four from flexor digitorum profundus, and one from flexor pollicis longus.
4. Describe findings and causes of a subacromial impingement.
Findings include a painful arc and positive impingement tests, typically caused by mechanical compression of the rotator cuff under the coracoacromial arch.
5. May cause atrophy of the thenar and hypothenar eminence of the hand.
Chronic nerve compression, severe denervation, or intrinsic muscle disease.
6. Indicate all structures found in the anatomical snuff box.
Radial artery, superficial branch of the radial nerve, and tendons of the extensor pollicis brevis, abductor pollicis longus, and extensor pollicis longus.
7. Prevents posterior dislocation of the clavicle.
The sternoclavicular and costoclavicular ligaments.
8. Differentiate between swan neck, mallet, Dupuytren’s, boutonniere’s pathologies
Swan neck (PIP hyperextension, DIP flexion); mallet (loss of extension at DIP); Dupuytren’s (palmar fascia contracture); boutonniere’s (PIP flexion, DIP hyperextension).
9. Mechanism of injury for glenohumeral dislocation. What nerve?
Forced abduction, external rotation, and extension; puts the axillary nerve at risk.
10. Define the scapulohumeral rhythm.
The synchronized kinematic movement ratio (typically 2:1) between the scapula and humerus during shoulder elevation.
11. Define torticollis. What muscle(s) is involved? How should you strengthen it?
Head-tilt posture involving the sternocleidomastoid (SCM); managed by stretching the tight side and strengthening the contralateral rotators.
12. Hyperextension of the PIPs and flexion of the DIPs
Swan neck deformity.
13. What is a joint aspiration? Why should it be done?
The removal of synovial fluid to relieve pressure or diagnose conditions like infection or crystal arthropathy.
14. Name the layers of the anterior forearm. What ms are found in each layer?
Superficial, intermediate, and deep layers holding flexor-pronator group muscles such as flexor carpi radialis, flexor digitorum superficialis, and flexor digitorum profundus.
15. What physical observation is noted with median nerve damage?
Ape hand deformity due to thenar muscle wasting and loss of thumb opposition.
16. Describe the capsular pattern for adhesive capsulitis. How should you mobilize the shoulder to improve flexion and abduction? What planes are involved in above?
Greatest loss in external rotation followed by abduction; mobilize with inferior and posterior glides in the sagittal and frontal planes.
17. Fracture of the distal radius.
A common fracture of the distal radius characterized by dorsal displacement (Colles' fracture).
18. Deformities of the hand (not fingers only) associated with nerve injuries.
Ape hand (median nerve), claw hand (ulnar/median nerve), and wrist drop (radial nerve).
19. Bouchard’s nodes are indicative of?
Osteoarthritis affecting the proximal interphalangeal (PIP) joints.
20. How would you palpate tendons of the cuff muscles?
Supraspinatus with arm extended/internally rotated, infraspinatus with external rotation, and subscapularis via passive external rotation.
21. Compression to the lower branches of the brachial plexus is known as_______ What is the mechanism of injury?
Thoracic Outlet Syndrome (C8-T1), often caused by cervical ribs, tight scalenes, or overhead postural compression.
22. “anterior tipping” of the scapula indicates tightness of this muscle
Pectoralis minor.
23. A lesion to the radial nerve at the level of the mid humerus would result in loss of ____
Wrist and finger extension, known as wrist drop.
24. The inability to raise the arm above 120 deg may indicate injury to the _____________ Why?
May indicate injury to the deltoid, rotator cuff, or spinal accessory nerve disrupting leverage and scapulohumeral rhythm.
25. What is the normal carrying angle for males, female ?
Typically 5 to 15 degrees of valgus, with females generally showing a slightly wider angle.
26. When should you perform a varus stress test?
To test the integrity of the lateral collateral ligament (LCL) for lateral joint instability.
27. Extension of the MCP with flexion of the IP’s
Claw hand (intrinsic minus) deformity.
28. What is the difference between an eccentric and concentric exercise?
Concentric involves muscle shortening under tension, while eccentric involves muscle lengthening under load.
29. Partial or complete severance of a nerve is known as ______ Can peripheral nerves regenerate? If so, how quickly?
Tmesis is complete nerve severance; peripheral nerves can regenerate at roughly 1 mm per day.
30. Name all of the scapulohumeral ms. Are they involved in uniaxial, biaxial or triaxial movements? If so, what plane and axes are involved?
Includes the deltoid, teres major, and rotator cuff muscles; they function as triaxial movers across multiple planes and axes.
31. Describe the dermatomes and myotomes for C5, C6, C7, C8, and T1.
C5 (lateral arm/shoulder abduction); C6 (lateral forearm/wrist extension); C7 (middle finger/elbow extension); C8 (medial forearm/finger flexion); T1 (medial arm/finger abduction).
32. How should you palpate the biceps and infraspinatus tendon?
Palpate the biceps tendon in the bicipital groove with slight external rotation, and the infraspinatus tendon just inferior to the posterolateral acromion.
33. Describe Golfers elbow and tennis elbow. What are the mechanisms of injury?
Golfer’s elbow (medial epicondylitis) involves repetitive wrist flexion/pronation; tennis elbow (lateral epicondylitis) involves repetitive wrist extension/gripping.
34. Define Froment’s sign.
A test for ulnar nerve palsy where the patient flexes the thumb IP joint to compensate for a weak adductor pollicis when holding paper.
35. Damage to the lower trunk of the brachial plexus would result in loss of or limitation to what movements? How does this affect the power grip?
Results in loss of intrinsic hand muscle function, severely impairing finger flexion and power grip stability.
36. Pain with flexion and adduction of the thumb would be indicative of_______. What is the name of the test to confirm the above findings? What ms would be involved?
Indicative of De Quervain's tenosynovitis, confirmed by Finkelstein's test, involving the abductor pollicis brevis and extensor pollicis brevis tendons.
37. Course of vertebral artery after entering the foramen magnum
It courses anteriorly along the ventral surface of the medulla to join the contralateral side and form the basilar artery.
38. Course of common carotid artery.
Ascends within the carotid sheath through the neck, branching into the internal and external carotids.
